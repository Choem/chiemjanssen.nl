Reef

An endless reef, grown by everyone who visits.

Dive in

0 m · The surface

A reef that never ends

Reef is one long coral reef seen from the side, projected big in a dark room. Walk along it and it just keeps going. Up top the water is warm and full of light, further down it turns blue and quiet, and at the very bottom lies the cold-water reef, where it is completely dark.

On the first day it is mostly empty sand. Every visitor adds an animal, so by the last day of a show the reef is full of life that people made together.

10 m · Your animal

Add your own animal

At the kiosk you answer a few playful questions, like how you spend a lazy Sunday. Out of your answers comes a real animal: a parrotfish, a moray eel, maybe a sea turtle. You give it a name, let it go, and it swims down to the depth where it really lives.

You take home a card with a QR code, so you can visit your animal again from the couch.

Or something rare. One in twenty visitors gets lucky: an endangered species, revealed in gold, with a golden card to match.

30 m · Explore

Go and explore

Drag the reef sideways and it keeps on going. Tap a fish, a coral or a sea star and it tells you who it is, where it lives and why it matters. Deeper down the light fades, and your finger becomes a lamp that shows what hides in the dark.

80 m · Weather

Day, night and weather

The reef keeps its own time. Mornings start misty, rain dimples the surface and storms stir up the water. At night the long-spined urchins come out, and every hour the lights dim and the corals spawn.

And if you are patient, something big swims past: a whale shark, a school of hammerheads, a humpback singing somewhere in the blue.

150 m · The damage

Not all of it is pretty

Some stretches of the reef are damaged, each by something that really happens in that kind of place. Tap one and it tells you what happened and what helps.

300 m · Where it fits

Where it fits

Festivals and galleries

On one wall or across a whole room. It grows visibly over the days of a show and runs on its own, and every visitor leaves with something of their own.

Schools and museums

Every animal is a real species at its real depth, with facts and sources on its card. The damage is real too, tied to real places. Pupils find their own animal back at home and keep exploring.
